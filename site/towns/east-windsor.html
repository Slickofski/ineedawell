What the snapshot covers for East Windsor

Built for your lot, not the town average.

Permits: new private wells in East Windsor are permitted through the North Central District Health Department (NCDHD). A well permit typically runs about $200 via the health district; verify with the district when the time comes.

Dry-run findings, Broad Brook area: 25 wells within 5 km of our test point, 14 with usable records: typical depths 140 to 425 ft at 8 to 30 gallons per minute (gpm).

Source: project dry run #1, East Windsor/Broad Brook, 2026-09-18.

A recent East Windsor listing we screened: 7 nearby wells within about 2 miles: 223 to 663 ft deep, 0.5 to 40 gpm yields.

Source: 193 Wells Rd permit-packet test run, 2026-09-18.

What we can say statewide (not East Windsor-specific): Connecticut's open well-completion dataset holds 8,205 reports: online submissions since January 2021. Older records are fragmented across towns, health districts, and unscanned files. Planning cost rates run $25 to $65/ft (2026 national guide ranges, planning context only), and every snapshot carries a +$10,000 to $25,000 dry-hole contingency, because the standard driller contract bills the attempt whether the hole comes up wet or dry.

Sources: CT DCP open well data; 2026 published national guide ranges.

Water quality: Connecticut requires arsenic and uranium testing for newly constructed private and semipublic wells (CT DPH, effective October 1, 2022). The snapshot flags what your area's geology suggests testing for: a lab test on your own well still has the final word.

Setbacks (CT top-lines): the legal distance a well must sit from septic and water: 75 ft well-to-septic at under 10 gallons per minute (gpm) (the band follows the well's required withdrawal rate, not its tested yield), 25 ft from a watercourse. Your snapshot lists the top-lines to confirm for your lot with the health district.
