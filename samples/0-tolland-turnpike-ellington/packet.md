## 4. Water-quality flags

- Water quality: the state panel covers it: Connecticut requires arsenic and uranium testing on every new well: the two contaminants that actually show up in CT bedrock: so the lab runs the state panel as routine. Radon is optional; ask the lab whether to add it, and get the panel price when you book.

- For a NEW well, Connecticut's required baseline test panel (15 tests: CGS 19a-37(c)(2), effective 2022-10-01) includes arsenic and uranium by law. Lead joins
  the required panel as a first-draw sample when the new well serves an EXISTING structure.
  For an existing well tested voluntarily, none of these are automatic: request them on
  the lab panel.
- Risk flags are not a prediction. Laboratory testing of the finished well is the final word.

## 5. Setback checklist (CT rules: your engineer confirms each distance on the site plan)

A setback is the legal distance a well must sit from septic and water.

State rules verified; North Central District Health Department (NCDHD) add-ons checked (Oct 2026): the ones that apply are included below.

| Separation | Minimum | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Well to septic system and its leaching field (where septic water drains into the soil), buried oil tank, or other pollution source | 75 ft | To confirm on the site plan |
| Well to surface water (high-water mark) / surface-water or foundation drain | 25 ft | To confirm on the site plan |
| Well to tight-joint sewer (extra-heavy cast iron, leaded joints, or equal) | 25 ft | To confirm on the site plan |
| Well to the leaching system (where septic water drains into the soil) IF the soil is fast AND the ledge is shallow: the distance doubles | 150 ft | To confirm on the site plan |
| Well to property boundary. District guidance: keep 25 ft 'if possible' (guidance, not a state setback) | 25 ft | To confirm on the site plan |

Screening-level only. Not a substitute for survey, wetlands delineation, or
field soil/perc tests. One rule can change this number: if the soil drains very fast (perc faster than 1 minute per inch) and ledge rock is shallow (leaching bottom less than 8 ft above it), the state doubles the well-to-septic distance: 150 ft for a typical home. That is only known after a perc test, which happens at permit time.

If a distance genuinely cannot be met, the paths are narrow and specific: DPH can grant an exception to the separating-distance requirements for a sewage system under CGS 19a-209c: affected well owners must be notified by certified mail, and an approval is not a defense against contamination claims, and the Commissioner holds limited exception authority under Public Health Code 19-13-B51m(c). There is no general rule letting a health director reduce setbacks; do not plan around one.
