How we got these numbers: the per-foot rates are planning points picked from published national cost guides, not quotes. Angi (updated September 18, 2026) puts residential well drilling at $25 to $65 per foot, and $50 to $75 per foot in rocky ground. CostHelper puts drilling with casing at $15 to $30 per foot, rising to $30 to $50 or more in difficult ground. The National Ground Water Association's Well Owners Guide reports drilling-only averages of about $24 per foot for a 4-inch well and about $30 per foot for a 6-inch well, with casing billed on top of that. We plan at $25, $45, and $65 per foot for drilling and casing. The pump, pressure tank, electrical work, and trenching are not in these numbers; drillers quote those separately. Your driller's written quote is the real number.

The hole is the middle of the bill: add pump, pressure tank, electrical, and trenching (quoted separately), plus the well permit (set by your health district: $200 in the North Central District, which covers East Windsor, Enfield, Ellington, Suffield, Vernon, and Windsor Locks; $150 in Bloomfield; check your district's current fee schedule). Dry-hole truth: one risk no guide can price for your lot: you pay for the attempt. Drillers bill per foot whether or not the hole produces, so a failed hole at the depths common around here can run about $9,000 to $18,000 at the plan-on rate, before you decide whether to deepen it, hydrofracture it, or drill again somewhere else. We carry a +$10,000–$25,000 contingency for that. It is our planning allowance, built from the rates above, not a published figure. Your three driller quotes are the real numbers; this band is what you walk in with.
